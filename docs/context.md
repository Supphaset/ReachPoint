## Foundation

Two human Data Scientists collaborate with AI development tools. Project setup, documentation, static design artifacts and online research are authorized. Product implementation, telephone/AI integration, paid services, infrastructure and deployment require separate explicit authorization. No real clinical partner, responder interview or firsthand disaster-response experience has been established.

The initial working name was Relief Ledger. On 2026-10-11 the user selected **ReachPoint** and requested a new repository; public visibility and a minimal main-branch bootstrap followed by a documentation review PR were explicitly chosen. See [name/publication decision 0008](decisions/0008-reachpoint-name-and-public-repository.md). Actual repository publication status belongs in the project entry point and working agreement.

## Product direction and changes

Early discussions emphasized report reconciliation, duplicate/conflicting reports, commitments and separate confirmed outcomes. Those remain supporting invariants; the user rejected quantity accounting as the central demonstration.

The accepted direction focuses on volunteer daily planning, coordinator emergency allocation and resident help timing. Team leaders choose areas and propose routine relief missions; coordinators review and assign emergency missions. The map combines dated flood evidence with distinct modeled exposure, reviewed needs, commitments and assessment gaps. Satellite/building estimates cannot prove current occupancy, trapped people or required evacuation.

The user requested coverage for people who do not contact the desk. The prototype includes a silent area and a scoped field-discovery report; partial assessment does not clear an entire area or establish universal coverage.

## Accepted bounded prototype

[Decision 0005](decisions/0005-prototype-scope-proposal.md) accepts five experiences: area map/daily briefing, area detail/mission planner, emergency desk, team mission and resident request/help status. One fictional locality, three planning areas, three declared synthetic team profiles and one connected demonstration bound v1.

The resident sees independently accepted assistance, a responsible estimated window or unknown, the last update and known changes. An unaccepted offer does not confirm arrival; a resource failure withdraws the stale current window. A next status-update commitment is distinct from arrival.

Real inbound telephone access remains requested and has its own feasibility gate. Browser voice cannot silently substitute for it. Qualified review in the prototype is explicitly synthetic; no autonomous clinical diagnosis, prescribing or triage is approved. No source-backed flood analysis or actual integration is established by static wireframes.
