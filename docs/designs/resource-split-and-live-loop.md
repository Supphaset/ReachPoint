## Problem Statement

During a flood, volunteer teams have far fewer boats, people and supplies than there are people needing help. In the user's words, ReachPoint should be "an informative system that lets the volunteer work efficiently and know who needs help the most and be able to split and help as many victims as possible with their limited resources."

Today that split is made by phone calls and chat groups. Teams overlap, urgent requests wait without an owner, and residents get no honest answer about when help comes. The hackathon build (Google Cloud AI Builder Cup 2026; official deadline 18 Oct 2026 with no published time or timezone, so the team's own deadline is **17 Oct 21:00 Bangkok**; two data scientists, 7 days) must show this working end to end on Google Cloud, with Gemini doing visible, meaningful work (40% of the score).

## What Makes This Cool

- **The split.** Three teams, one boat (S1, 12 passenger movements a day), 80 food packs and three areas competing for them. ReachPoint suggests who goes where to help the most people, explains why in plain Thai or English, and lets a human approve it.
- **The failure.** S1 breaks down at 10:30. The plan re-solves and the coordinator approves the new split. The resident's stale 13:00–14:00 window disappears, replaced by "on hold, next update 11:00". The unrelated relief mission stays untouched. Map, desk, team screen and resident page all change within seconds.
- **"What would one more boat change?"** The same solver, run with a hypothetical extra resource, shows how many more people a second boat would reach versus more food packs. The coordinator can use it to ask for the right help. It is labelled as a simulation and never reaches the resident.
- **Real satellite water under it.** The map shows the Nov 2025 Hat Yai flood extent, computed from Sentinel-1 (23 Nov 2025 pass, checked against the EOS/AIT maps; urban areas may be under-detected). See pack §5.1c.
- **Who may be missing.** Each zone briefing shows an estimated range of people living in the flooded part (Open Buildings + WorldPop, cross-checked with DOPA registered population) next to the reviewed count, e.g. "About 1,400–1,900 live here (estimate) · 9 reported needing evacuation". The estimate never creates a case and never adds to counts.

## Premises

Agreed after three rounds; the user corrected rounds 1 and 2.

1. **The product helps the most people with limited resources.** Volunteers see who needs help most and why. The system suggests how to split teams, boats and supplies so more people get helped and teams don't overlap. The live loop keeps every request owned with honest timing, and replans when a resource is lost.
2. **Priority is explained, not an AI score.** "Needs help most" comes from visible reasons: reviewed medical need (from the qualified review only), evacuation demand, people still without supplies, and time waiting. The weights are shown and adjustable. Gemini explains the ranking; it never sets it.
3. **Gemini works for everyone, proposal-only.**
   - Residents: Thai voice intake that proposes facts with transcript quotes.
   - Volunteers: a mission briefing (why here, what to bring, what's unknown) and voice/photo field reports turned into proposed events.
   - Coordinator: change summaries.
4. **Google products only where they do real work.** That now includes Google OR-Tools for the split.
5. **No real phone line in the prototype** (0009 amendments); the browser call screen represents a call and is disclosed.
6. **Cut order if behind:** Bangkok package → reconciliation UI polish → **coordinator copilot's replan drafting** (keep its read-only Q&A) → whole coordinator copilot (the demo falls back to Approach B: solver + Gemini explanation, no agent) → P-C field-report scene → Open Buildings counts → real phone line. The split suggestion and replanning are never cut.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Ranked list, manual split.** No optimizer. Rejected: nothing actually helps teams split scarce resources, which is the thesis.
- **B: OR-Tools split + Gemini explanation.** Solver-suggested split with priority floors, `unmet` as a valid result, re-solve on resource loss, and the simulated "one more boat" comparison. Gemini explains.

## Recommended Approach

**C, built so that the second agent doesn't double the risk:**

1. **One agent framework for both agents: Google Agent Development Kit (ADK, Python).**
   - The resident intake agent uses ADK's bidirectional streaming with the Gemini Live model, for browser voice now and the phone bridge later.
   - The coordinator copilot is a text ADK agent on a Gemini Flash model.
   - Both run inside the same FastAPI service on Cloud Run and share one tool layer.
   - **Voice fallback ladder (does not depend on ADK Live):**
     1. ADK Live streaming.
     2. **Push-to-talk:** record a clip in the browser, send the audio to Gemini Flash multimodal, get the same quote-linked proposed events back. Still Gemini, still Thai.
     3. Text chat.
   - **Switch criterion, checked Wed 14 Oct 12:00 Bangkok:** three scripted synthetic Thai test sessions must each produce the correct landmark, people count and assistance types as proposals, with no session drop over 10 s. If not, ship rung 2 and record why in the deck.
   - **Ownership:**
     - Friend: the intake agent, the ADK runtime inside FastAPI, the shared tool layer and the rules engine.
     - User: the solver (`allocation.py`, a pure function `solve_split(snapshot) -> suggestion` with no I/O, which the friend's API calls and persists), the shared priority tiers and sort (`tiers.py`), the coordinator split panel with "Ask these teams" and What-if, the Gemini briefing/split-explanation prompts, and the coordinator copilot agent definition (instructions and tool choice) built on the friend's tool layer. Full table: [delivery agreement](../collaboration/two-person-delivery-agreement.md#who-owns-what).
     - Interface boundary: the `AllocationInput` and `AllocationSuggestion` JSON Schemas in `contract/`.
2. **Tools are the only way agents touch data, and every tool is read-only or proposal-only.** For example:
   - `get_area_briefing`, `get_case`
   - `propose_event` (writes to `proposedEvents` only)
   - `solve_split(scenario)` (returns a suggestion, writes nothing authoritative)
   - `compare_scenarios(extra_resource)` (labelled as simulation)

   Authoritative changes still go through the rules engine after a human clicks approve.
3. **Split solver: OR-Tools CP-SAT in `api/rules/allocation.py`, owned by the user.**
   - **Inputs:** a versioned snapshot of reviewed needs, teams, resources and existing commitments.
   - **Variables:** team/resource × need-scope assignments, each optional.
   - **Hard constraints:** capability fit; capacity (S1 = 6 seats × 2 trips = 12 passenger movements); accepted allocations stay fixed in the snapshot (pull model, 21A: nothing is reserved before a team accepts). An accepted allocation commits **only its claimed groups** (the specific household groups and assistance type) **and the capacity it consumes**. The rest of that need and the rest of that resource's capacity stay assignable. No person-assistance pair or capacity unit is double-booked.
   - Accepted allocations and listing revisions are part of `inputVersions`, so a claim or correction during review makes the suggestion stale.
   - **Urgent tier (tier 1)** contains only needs with `review = validated` that also have either:
     - a qualified-review fixture requiring urgent medical capability (C-01's medical need), or
     - a coordinator `urgent` flag recorded with a stated reason during operational review (for example an evacuation linked to a medical case, as with C-01's transport).

     Other assisted-evacuation demand (the rest of P-B's 24) and supply needs are tier 2. Inside tier 1, needs with a qualified medical requirement come first (objective level 1a), then other tier-1 needs (1b). Waiting time only breaks ties (level 3).
   - **Objective (lexicographic, in this order):**
     - (1a) maximise persons covered whose tier-1 need has a qualified urgent medical requirement;
     - (1b) maximise other tier-1 persons covered;
     - (2) maximise the weighted tier-2 person-units covered, `w_evac × evacuation units + w_supply × supply units` (defaults 1.0 / 1.0);
     - (3) minimise total waiting time of uncovered needs, as a strict last tie-break.

     So a 3-person urgent evacuation can never displace C-01's qualified medical need, and waiting time never trades against coverage.
   - **Person-unit** = one person for one assistance type. Each reviewed person counts at most once **per assistance type**. Covering a person's tier-1 need (for example medical) does not remove their value for a *different* assistance type in tier 2 (for example food); the same person and the same assistance type are never counted twice. Partial coverage counts the covered persons only (6 of 24 evacuees = 6). Counts come from reviewed `caseId`/cohort records, never from reports, so a repeated call cannot add persons.
   - **Adjustable weights** (shown in the UI) are only `w_evac` and `w_supply` in level (2), plus the ranked list display. Waiting time is not weighted. No setting can move a need into or out of tier 1, or let tier 2 displace a coverable tier-1 need.
   - **Unassigned needs are a valid output**, each with a reason (`no_capable_team`, `capacity_exhausted`, `access_unknown`).
   - **Output:** suggestion ID, input versions, assignments, unassigned needs with reasons, and per-need explanation fields for Gemini. It writes nothing authoritative.
3a. **Pull model: from reviewed case to authoritative records (rules engine, owned by the friend; 21A + eng re-check D6–D13):**
   1. **Publish.** A reviewed case becomes a **listing** per case × assistance type, with `needRevision`, `lastConfirmedAt` and its **household groups**. Each household from one call is one "together" group by default; only the coordinator can split a group (D6). The listing appears in the server-written open list for its assistance type (evacuation / medical / supplies). Every team reads the lists it is qualified for, so a team that turns Available later still sees every waiting case (D10). Resident views say "seeking a suitable team; arrival time is not confirmed".
   2. **Order.** Lists are sorted by visible priority colour (RED life-threatening / YELLOW urgent / GREEN can wait, mapped from the shared tiers in `api/rules/tiers.py`), then distance from the team, then waiting time. RED always comes first (D9). **Colour mapping (proposed 12 Oct by the user, friend to confirm):** RED = tier 1 (1a: a qualified review requiring urgent medical capability; 1b: a coordinator `urgent` flag with a stated reason, e.g. C-01's transport); YELLOW = any other validated evacuation or medical need; GREEN = validated supplies. Unknown distance (unlocated pickup, or no team location) sorts after every known distance within its colour; the listing ID breaks the last ties. Free text never changes the order, only reviewed fields do. Each row shows the distance and "last confirmed HH:MM". A team can ask the desk to re-confirm before leaving (D8).
   3. **Claim.** A team taps "Take" on one or more whole groups (`claimListing`, sending the `needRevision` it saw). In one Firestore transaction over the listing, resource and team, the engine checks capability, Available status, remaining seats or stock, no overlap with already-claimed groups, and the revision. Results: an allocation in state `accepted`, or `ALREADY_TAKEN`, `NOT_ENOUGH_CAPACITY` or `NEED_CHANGED` ("This case changed, please look again"). Any review correction, withdrawal or group change updates the listing in the same transaction (D8).
   4. **Window.** The team sets its window. Only then does the resident see it, **per group** ("Family 1: boat on the way 13:00–14:00 · Family 3: still looking for a team"); the case shows "partly covered" (D6).
   5. **Progress and endings (D7).** accepted → en route → on scene → closed as **Done / Already left or helped by others / Not found / Needs more help**. With "Needs more help", only the remainder is relisted as a new revision. At the scene the team reports who it actually took. The team lead or the coordinator can **hand back** unfinished groups before Done; only those groups return to the list. Supplies marked "delivered, not confirmed" are never relisted automatically; the coordinator decides. A closed allocation rejects late updates (`ALLOCATION_CLOSED`), and taking a case again creates a new allocation.
   6. **Urgent first without timers (D2).** RED listings are pinned. A team turning Available gets a one-time "urgent case waiting" prompt. The coordinator can send a direct "Can you take this?" ask, which is how the solver's suggested split is acted on (D12).
3b. **Allocations that depend on a failed resource:** when the coordinator reviews a `resource_unavailable` report (for example S1), the rules engine, in the same transaction:
   - moves every **accepted** allocation using it to `held` (below), or closes it as handed back for the groups not yet done;
   - relists the unfinished groups as a new listing revision;
   - marks any resident message tied to those allocations as "No longer current" and shows "Update needed" on the desk (D11);
   - sets the resource's capacity to 0 in the next snapshot.

   The re-solve then sees no dead boat as committed and stays feasible, possibly with more unmet need, each with a reason.
3c. **Hold on resource failure:** when the coordinator reviews a `resource_unavailable` report, the review form requires a next-update time.
   - The default is now + 30 minutes; the coordinator can edit it.
   - The reviewing coordinator becomes the next-update owner.
   - If that time passes with no new update, the desk shows the case as **update overdue** in red, and the resident sees "Still on hold. An update is overdue; the coordinator desk has been alerted." No new time is invented.
4. **Gemini usage map:**
   - Gemini Live (ADK streaming): Thai intake.
   - Gemini Flash structured output: proposed events, field-report extraction from voice or photo, and briefing/explanation text grounded only in computed fields.
   - Coordinator copilot (ADK).
5. **Google product map:**
   - Agent Development Kit, Gemini via Vertex AI
   - Cloud Run, Firestore, Firebase Auth/Hosting
   - Google Maps JS, Earth Engine (offline precompute), Cloud Storage, Secret Manager
   - OR-Tools, plus Cloud Logging for agent traces in the demo

## Success Criteria

- On the deployed URL, the coordinator sees a suggested split for P-A/P-B/P-C with per-need reasons. No urgent reviewed need is left unassigned while an easier need is covered, unless capacity makes it impossible, and then the reason says so.
- After S1 becomes unavailable and the coordinator approves the replan:
  - M-01 is held, the resident window is withdrawn, and the next-update owner is visible.
  - The new suggestion never exceeds remaining capacity.
  - PL-A is unchanged.
- "One more boat" shows a different covered count and is labelled simulated. Nothing in resident or team views changes from it.
- The ADK copilot answers "what changed since 10:00?" using only tool results, with record IDs cited, and cannot apply a change without a human approval click.
- These invariant checks pass as automated tests:
  - no double count on replay
  - capacity never exceeded
  - no window before acceptance
  - stale window removed on failure
  - **approving a stale suggestion returns `VERSION_CONFLICT` and writes nothing**
  - two teams claiming the same group: one wins; two teams claiming different groups: both win; a claim on an old `needRevision` returns `NEED_CHANGED`
  - a closed allocation rejects late updates; a hand-back relists only the unfinished groups
  - the solver never counts one person twice for one assistance type
  - no weight setting lets a tier-2 need displace a coverable tier-1 need

### R3-1 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Open `offered` missions now stay fixed until they are declined or expire. But nothing covers an open offer whose resource fails, for example an offer on S1 when S1 breaks at 10:30. **Remedy**

> Define what happens to open offers (and fixed accepted commitments) that depend on a resource reported `resource_unavailable`. ### Pass 7: Unresolved decisions (5 resolved, 0 open)

**Pull-based priority list (21A, user model, supersedes push offers).**
- **Who is asked.** A reviewed case is published to every team that has the needed capability and has declared itself available. Boat teams get evacuation; the medical team gets medical.
- **First to accept wins.** The first team to tap "We'll take it" gets the case, applied in a Firestore transaction. A second tap shows "Already taken by T-S".
- **No answer means not available.** "Can't take it" or no answer both mean the team isn't available. There are no timers and nothing expires.
- **The backlog.** Untaken cases stay in a shared **priority list**: RED/YELLOW/GREEN first, then nearest (D9). Any team that becomes free later sees it at once (D10) and can take a case from it.
- **Unmet cases stay visible.** They are never hidden, and each carries its reason.
- **Resident updates.** When a team accepts and sets its window, the resident's status updates. The coordinator can post a status update to the resident at any time; it is labelled as an update, not an arrival time.
- **Solver role (updated by eng re-check D12).** The solver's split is shown only to the coordinator (split panel and What-if), who acts on it with direct asks. Team lists show no "Best fit" chips. Any capable team may take any case.
- **Changes to earlier decisions.**
  - 3A's button becomes **"Ask these N teams"** (direct asks; publishing happens on review).
  - Nothing is reserved before acceptance, so the 15-minute offer expiry (part of eng D26), the expiry badge on the team screen, and the displacement of lower-priority offers (eng D25) no longer apply.
  - The eng re-check (2026-10-11) settled the allocation lifecycle; see "Eng re-check decisions" below.

### Eng re-check decisions (/plan-eng-review, 2026-10-11, pull model)
Backed by real-practice research: `docs/research/real-rescue-practice.md`.
- **D1/D6 Partial by family.** A household from one call is a together group by default. Teams take whole groups; only the coordinator splits a group. The team reports actual pickups at the scene. The resident page shows status per group.
- **D33 Seat units (2026-10-12, user, proposed, friend to confirm).** Boats and trucks count seat units, not heads: adult 1, child under 12 half, baby on a lap 0, stretcher or wheelchair 3. A team leader can override the values for their own vehicle. The intake records each household's adults, children, babies and stretchers (`party`).
- **D34 Safe split suggestions (2026-10-12, user, proposed, friend to confirm; extends D6).** The split solver may suggest splitting a family only when that fits more people. A part never goes, and never waits, with a child, a baby or a stretcher case but no adult; both parts go to the same shelter. Each split is a suggestion the coordinator approves (the existing coordinator group split); teams still take whole groups.
- **D35 Waiting and weights (2026-10-12, user, proposed, friend to confirm).** The waiting tie-break counts person-minutes. Each weight stays at 0.1 or more (a zero weight could leave a seat empty while people wait). Tier-2 medical needs use `w_evac`.
- **D36 Stretcher cases (2026-10-12, user: prompt, not automatic; friend to confirm).** Seat units alone push a bedridden person behind families that fit more people (notebook run, C-05). The desk prompts the coordinator for an urgent flag with a reason ("bedridden, can't self-evacuate") whenever a request includes a stretcher or wheelchair, which makes it tier 1b.
- **D2 Urgent first, no timers.** RED is pinned, there is a one-time prompt on becoming Available, and the coordinator can send a direct "Can you take this?" ask.
- **D3 Ordering.** One deterministic sort shares its tiers with the solver (`api/rules/tiers.py`); publishing never waits for the solver.
- **D4/D13 Team status.** Available / Busy / Off duty, with the time it was set and an optional reason (refuelling, boat repair, rest, on another job) and "back about HH:MM". It never changes automatically.
- **D5/D11 Resident messages.** Gated templates labelled "Message from the coordinator desk". The server fills team and time from the live job; notes are chosen from preset phrases (no free text). A message tied to a released or failed job shows "No longer current", and the desk shows "Update needed".
- **D7 Job endings.** Done / Already left or helped by others / Not found / Needs more help (the remainder is relisted). Hand-back by the lead or coordinator returns only unfinished groups. "Delivered, not confirmed" supplies need the coordinator. Closed jobs reject late updates.
- **D8 Outdated cases.** "Last confirmed HH:MM", an "Ask desk to re-confirm" button, and a need-revision check on every claim.
- **D9 Distance.** Colour first, then nearest, then waiting time. RED always comes first.
- **D10 Shared open lists** per assistance type, so teams that become free later see every waiting case.
- **D12 Solver.** Coordinator only (split panel + What-if); no per-team Best fit chips.
- **D14 TODO.** Import and de-duplicate requests from jitasa.care / Traffy Fondue / LINE channels after the hackathon.

**Other decisions:**
- **Weights (17A).** Only the coordinator changes weights, and they apply on an explicit "Recalculate". Leaders see the current weights read-only, with who set them and when.
- **Split visibility (18A).** The coordinator sees the full panel (Publish, weights, What if). Team leaders see a read-only view with their own team highlighted, the priority list, and "We'll take it" on cases they can serve.
- **"Other" window (19A).** Start/end time wheels in 30-minute steps (maximum 3 hours), plus "Not sure yet", which keeps the resident window at "time not confirmed yet".
- **Offer countdown (20).** Superseded by 21A: there are no timers.
